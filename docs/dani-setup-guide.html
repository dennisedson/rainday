Rainy Day Merchandise

Setting up your order notifications

Four short tasks that make your shop tell you when something sells, and stop it selling things you've run out of.

Time
About 20 minutes
You'll need
HubSpot & Square admin access
Send back
One access key
01

Create an access key

In HubSpot

This is the password your website uses to write orders into HubSpot. Nobody sees it but the site.

  1. Open Settings (the gear, top right) → Integrations → Private Apps.
  2. Click Create a private app. Name it Rainy Day Website so you recognise it later.
  3. Open the Scopes tab. Scopes are permissions. Search for each one below and tick it.
    Tick exactly these five
    • crm.objects.contacts.read
    • crm.objects.contacts.write
    • crm.objects.deals.read
    • crm.objects.deals.write
    • crm.schemas.deals.write
  4. Click Create app, then Continue creating when it asks you to confirm.
  5. Copy the token. It starts with pat-na1- and is about 44 characters.

    Send it to Dennis somewhere private — a text message rather than email, and delete it once he confirms it works.

If a scope won't tick, your seat may not have permission to grant it. That's a HubSpot account-level setting, not something you've done wrong — you'd need a Super Admin to either grant it or create the app for you.

02

Add three order fields

In HubSpot

Right now a sale creates a deal that says only a name and an amount. These three fields are what let it also tell you what was bought and where it's going.

  1. Go to Settings → Properties, then switch the object dropdown to Deal.
  2. Click Create property and make each of the three below.
  3. The internal name has to match exactly. HubSpot creates it from the label, so after typing the label, click the small </> or Edit link beside it and set the internal name yourself.
    Internal nameLabel to showField type
    order_itemsOrder ItemsMulti-line text
    shipping_addressShipping AddressMulti-line text
    square_receipt_urlSquare Receipt URLSingle-line text

The internal name is the part that matters. The label is just what you see on screen and you can change it any time. If an internal name is even slightly different — order_item instead of order_items — the website can't write to it, and orders will arrive with those boxes empty.

03

Get an email when someone orders

In HubSpot

Every sale already creates a deal and assigns it to you. This tells HubSpot to email you when that happens.

  1. Go to Settings → Objects → Deals, then open the Pipelines tab.
  2. Choose your pipeline (it's probably called Sales Pipeline) and open the Automate tab.
  3. Find the first stage — the one new deals land in, usually Appointment Scheduled. Click the automation icon next to it.
  4. Add Send internal email notification and put yourself as the recipient.

    Keep the subject short and recognisable — something like New order on the website.

  5. Turn the automation on and save. Test it by placing a small order on the site.

Why not the "deal assigned to me" notification? You can switch that on in Profile & Preferences → Notifications, and it's worth doing as a backup. But it usually only fires when a deal's owner changes, and your orders arrive already assigned to you — so it may stay silent. The pipeline automation above fires on the sale itself, which is why it's the one to set up first.

04

Let the site track your stock

In Square

You're currently marking things unavailable by hand. That works — the website respects it. But it means remembering after every sale.

If you turn on stock tracking instead, the site counts down as things sell and marks an item Sold Out on its own the moment you hit zero. Nobody can buy something you can't post.

  1. Open Square Dashboard → Items & Orders → Items, and click an item you want to track.
  2. Turn on Track inventory (Square sometimes calls this Manage stock).
  3. Enter how many you actually have and save.
  4. Repeat for anything you make in limited numbers. Do a couple first and watch what happens before doing them all.

Items without tracking stay buyable forever. That's deliberate — switching this on can't accidentally empty your whole shop. It only applies to items you've explicitly turned it on for, so there's no risk in trying it on one thing.

When you're done

  • Send Dennis the access key from task 1.
  • Tell him whether all five scopes ticked, or which ones wouldn't.
  • Place a small test order and check that you get an email and that the deal shows the items and address.

Tasks 1 to 3 need doing before the new site goes live. Task 4 you can do whenever — including after launch.
