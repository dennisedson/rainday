Running your shop

Where everything lives, and which of the two systems to open when you want to change something.

The one rule worth knowing

Your shop runs on two systems, and almost every question comes down to which one owns the thing you want to change.

If it is a thing you sell, change it in Square. If it is how the site looks, change it in HubSpot. Products never get created in HubSpot — the website reads them from Square automatically.

Editing a page

Every page is built from blocks called modules. Open the page in HubSpot, click a module, change its fields, publish.

HubSpot → Content → Website Pages → open a page → click a module → Publish
PageWhat you can change there
HomeHero image and headline, category tiles, your story section, testimonials
ShopCover photos, section title, how many columns, sort order
ProductLayout only — the product itself comes from Square
Cart & checkoutLayout only. Avoid editing these; they handle payments
FooterShop name, logo, tagline

Cover photos

Banner images work differently depending on the page, which is why the Shop All page felt broken.

The Shop All page

Open the Shop page, click the Category Banner module, and use the Shop All Cover Photo field.

This is the one that wouldn't change before. Shop All isn't a Square category, so it has no category photo to inherit — changing anything in Square will never affect it. It now has its own field, which is the only place that image comes from.

A category page — Bracelets, Earrings, and so on

Set the category image in Square. The banner picks it up on its own, and this is the normal way to change one.

To override Square for a single category, add an entry under Category Custom Content and match the Category Name exactly — Bracelets, not bracelets.

A category with no photo in Square

Use the Fallback Cover Photo field. Without it those banners render empty. Keychains has no Square photo today.

Theme settings

Under Design → Themes you'll find Brand Color, which you can change freely, and Square Environment.

Leave Square Environment set to "production". Switching it to sandbox points your live shop at Square's test system — orders would look successful and no money would arrive.

Products and photos

Everything you sell lives in Square. The website reads it and updates within about five minutes.

Square → Items & Orders → Items

An item's name, description, price, and photo all flow straight to the website. To hide something without deleting it, mark it unavailable — the site respects that immediately.

A category's photo becomes the banner on that category's page, which is how you change those cover images.

Stock

Turn on stock tracking for anything you make in limited numbers, and the shop counts down as things sell.

Square → Items & Orders → Items → open an item → Track inventory → enter your count

When a tracked item hits zero, the website shows Sold Out, disables the Add to Cart button, and refuses the purchase even if someone had the page open beforehand. People can still favourite it.

Items without tracking stay buyable forever. That's deliberate — turning this on can't accidentally empty your shop. It only applies to items you switch it on for, so try one first.

Sales tax

Kansas customers are charged tax. Customers in other states are not, because you don't owe tax elsewhere until you sell a great deal into that state.

Square → Settings → Account & Settings → Payments → Sales taxes

Your rule is called Kansas Jewelry. Change the percentage there and the website uses the new rate immediately — nothing needs redeploying.

The rate is currently 6.5%, which is the Kansas state rate on its own. Your county and city add more on top, so at 6.5% you collect less than you owe and make up the difference at filing time. Worth confirming your full combined rate with whoever does your taxes, then updating it here.

Shipping

The shipping fee is stored as the price of an item called Shipping in your catalog.

Square → Items & Orders → Items → Shipping → set the price

Customers never see it as a product — the website hides it and shows the amount as "Shipping" on the receipt instead. It's one flat charge per order, not per item, so a two-bracelet order pays it once.

Don't delete the Shipping item. It looks like a stray product in your list, but it's where the fee is stored. Deleting it silently makes shipping free. It's priced at $0 right now, so nothing is charged until you set an amount.

Receipts

Square emails a receipt to every customer automatically, and it's their only confirmation. Check it's switched on under Settings → Payments → Receipts.

What changed

The shop moved to new hosting, four things that were quietly broken got fixed, and five features were added.

Fixed

Orders never reached HubSpot

Not one order had ever created a record, since the shop was built. The website was trying to save details into fields that didn't exist, HubSpot rejected every attempt, and the error was being thrown away silently. This is why you couldn't see orders, addresses, or customer contact details. Orders now arrive with what was bought, where it ships, and a link to the receipt.

Fixed

Anyone could set their own price

The checkout accepted whatever total the browser sent, so a $200 order could have been paid as one cent. Square now prices every order from your catalog and the site charges what Square says.

Fixed

The menu sometimes came up empty

Your categories loaded in one batch that wasn't big enough, so a visitor arriving at a quiet moment could see a shop with no navigation at all.

Fixed

The Shop All cover photo

The page you told us about. It had no way to be changed — see the website tab for where its field now lives.

New

Kansas sales tax

Kansas customers are charged tax; other states aren't. You control the rate in Square.

New

Shipping fee

A flat charge per order, set by you in Square, currently $0 until you choose an amount.

New

Sold out actually means sold out

Out-of-stock items can no longer be bought — not from a stale page, not by anyone poking at the site directly. Works on items where you've turned tracking on.

New

Shipping addresses in Square

Orders now carry the delivery address, so Square's Orders tab shows you where each one is going.

New

Order details in HubSpot

Each sale creates a record listing the items, the shipping address, and the total, assigned to you so HubSpot can alert you.

Under the hood

New hosting

The part of the shop that talks to Square moved to Cloudflare — faster, no cold starts, and on a plan that permits selling. The old host stays available for a short while as a safety net.

Under the hood

Automatic checks

75 automated tests now run on every change, and updates deploy themselves once they pass. Any change can be undone in about two minutes.
